Pilgrim's Path - Kumano Kodo
============================

AW3D30.OSM - Kumano Kodo TOPO v__version__

An offline map for Garmin / Android / iOS.

## Map Coverage

* Latitude range: N33.0 ~ N35.0
* Longitude range: E135.0 ~ E137.0

### Famous Peaks

* Ōmine Mountains (the core of the Kii Mountain Range, a sacred Shugendō training ground)
  * Mount Hakkyo (1,915m) - the highest peak in the Kinki region, a Hyakumeizan
  * Sanjō-ga-take / Mount Ōmine (1,719m) - a sacred Shugendō mountain; women are still barred from the summit, where Ōminesan-ji temple stands
  * Mount Inamura (1,726m) - known as the "Women's Ōmine," open to female pilgrims

* Ōdaigahara Range
  * Mount Ōdaigahara (1,695m) - a Hyakumeizan and one of Japan's highest recorded annual-rainfall sites
  * Osugidani - one of Japan's three great gorges, linking Ōdaigahara to the Miya River

* Around the Kumano Sanzan
  * Nachi-san - the mountain area home to Nachi Falls and Kumano Nachi Taisha
  * The hills around Kumano Hongū - peaks along the Nakahechi route

### Famous Trekking Routes

  * The long-distance pilgrimage routes covered by this map (inscribed together with Koyasan and Yoshino-Ōmine as the UNESCO World Heritage "Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range" in 2004):
    * Nakahechi: the traditional route of pilgrims from Kyoto and Osaka, linking Tanabe to the Kumano Sanzan; has the densest concentration of oji (subsidiary shrines) along the way
    * Kohechi: links Koyasan to Kumano Hongū, running mostly along mountain trails; the most physically demanding of the pilgrimage routes
    * Ohechi: follows the southwestern coastline of the Kii Peninsula, connecting Tanabe to Nachi with views over the Pacific
    * Iseji North: links Ise Grand Shrine to Kumano, passing many stone-paved sections of old road (Matsumoto Pass, Magose Pass, etc.)
    * Iseji Central: the mountainous middle section of the Iseji route, via Owase and Kada
    * Iseji South: the southern section of the Iseji route, joining the Kumano Sanzan
    * Ōmine Okugake-michi: a Shugendō ridge-traverse pilgrimage route linking Yoshino to Kumano Hongū along the crest of the Ōmine Mountains; the most arduous of the Kii Mountain Range pilgrimage routes

### Famous Sights

* Kumano Sanzan: Kumano Hongū Taisha, Kumano Hayatama Taisha, and Kumano Nachi Taisha - visiting all three shrines is the core of Kumano pilgrimage worship
* Nachi Falls: a 133m single drop, Japan's tallest single-tier waterfall, standing beside Nachi Taisha and Seiganto-ji temple
* Koyasan: head temple complex of the Shingon sect, founded by Kūkai; the Danjō Garan and Okunoin are must-visit sites
* Yunomine Onsen: said to have been in use for over 1,800 years, one of Japan's oldest hot springs; inscribed alongside the pilgrimage routes as UNESCO World Heritage in 2004
* Doro-kyo: a gorge of striking rock formations carved by a tributary of the Kumano River, best seen by sightseeing boat
* Hashigui-iwa: a line of rock pillars along the coast that resemble bridge piers sinking into the sea

### Historical Events

* Heian-era imperial pilgrimages to Kumano: retired emperors such as Shirakawa and Go-Shirakawa each made the pilgrimage to the Kumano Sanzan on foot as many as 34 times; the unbroken procession of nobles and commoners alike became known as the "ant pilgrimage to Kumano."
* 7th century - Founding of Shugendō: tradition holds that En no Gyōja, founder of Shugendō, established a training ground on Mount Ōmine, laying the foundation for the Kii Mountains as a sacred site of ascetic practice.
* 816 - Founding of Koyasan: Kūkai (Kōbō Daishi), founder of the Shingon sect, was granted land by Emperor Saga to establish Kongōbu-ji on Koyasan, which remains the head temple of Shingon Esoteric Buddhism to this day.
* 2004 - "Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range" inscribed as UNESCO World Heritage: one of the few cases worldwide where the roads themselves are recognized as the cultural heritage.
* 2015 - Kumano Kodo and the Camino de Santiago become sister pilgrimage routes: the world's only two UNESCO-recognized pilgrimage routes formalized a sister relationship, launching a dual-stamp passport that encourages pilgrims to walk both.
