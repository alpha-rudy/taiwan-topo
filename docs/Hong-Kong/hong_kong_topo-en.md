Hong Kong Region
================

AW3D30.OSM - Hong-Kong TOPO v__version__

## Map Coverage

* Latitude range: N22.130 ~ N22.569
* Longitude range: E113.813 ~ E114.506

This map covers all of Hong Kong — Hong Kong Island, Kowloon, the New Territories, and the outlying islands including Lantau — with full coverage of the four major long-distance trails: the MacLehose, Wilson, Hong Kong, and Lantau Trails.

### Famous Peaks

1. New Territories and Kowloon
    * Tai Mo Shan (957m) - the highest peak in Hong Kong
    * Ma On Shan (702m)
    * Kowloon Peak / Fei Ngo Shan (602m) - the highest peak in Kowloon
    * Pat Sin Leng (main peak Shun Yeung Fung, 590m) - a famous ridge of eight connected peaks
    * Castle Peak (583m) - one of the "Three Sharp Peaks" of Hong Kong
    * Lion Rock (495m) - symbol of the "Lion Rock Spirit"

2. Lantau Island
    * Lantau Peak (934m) - the second-highest peak in Hong Kong, a renowned sunrise viewpoint
    * Sunset Peak (869m) - the third-highest peak in Hong Kong, famed for its autumn silvergrass
    * Nei Lak Shan (751m)

3. Hong Kong Island
    * Victoria Peak (552m) - the highest peak on Hong Kong Island, overlooking Victoria Harbour
    * Mount Parker (532m)

4. Sai Kung
    * Sharp Peak (468m) - the foremost of the "Three Sharp Peaks", steep and strikingly pointed
    * High Junk Peak (344m) - one of the "Three Sharp Peaks"

### Famous Trekking Routes

1. MacLehose Trail (about 100km in 10 stages, from Pak Tam Chung in Sai Kung to Tuen Mun; opened in 1979 as Hong Kong's first long-distance trail, and named by National Geographic among the world's dream hiking trails)
2. Wilson Trail (about 78km in 10 stages, from Stanley Gap on Hong Kong Island to Nam Chung in the New Territories, spanning the whole territory)
3. Hong Kong Trail (about 50km in 8 stages, from The Peak to Big Wave Bay; its final stage, the Dragon's Back, has been hailed as one of Asia's best urban hikes)
4. Lantau Trail (about 70km in 12 stages, a circuit from Mui Wo around Lantau Island over Lantau Peak and Sunset Peak)
5. Sharp Peak and the Tai Long Wan beaches (a classic Sai Kung East Country Park route combining Hong Kong's most rugged peak with its most beautiful beaches)

### Famous Sights

* Victoria Harbour and the night view from Victoria Peak
* Hexagonal volcanic rock columns at the High Island Reservoir East Dam (Hong Kong UNESCO Global Geopark)
* The Tai Long Wan "four beaches and one peak" (Sai Wan, Ham Tin Wan, Tai Wan, Tung Wan, and Sharp Peak)
* Dragon's Back (Shek O Peninsula, stage 8 of the Hong Kong Trail)
* Tian Tan Big Buddha and Po Lin Monastery (Ngong Ping, Lantau Island)
* Ng Tung Chai Waterfalls (northern slopes of Tai Mo Shan, the tallest waterfall group in Hong Kong)
* Bride's Pool waterfalls and Plover Cove Reservoir (Pat Sin Leng Country Park)
* Mai Po Nature Reserve (a Ramsar Wetland of International Importance and migratory bird haven)
* Tap Mun, Kat O, and Lai Chi Wo (outlying island scenery and historic Hakka villages)

### Historical Events

* 1841-1898 - the founding and cession of colonial Hong Kong: after the Qing dynasty's defeat in the First Opium War, Hong Kong Island was ceded to Britain under the 1842 Treaty of Nanking, the Kowloon Peninsula followed under the 1860 Convention of Peking, and in 1898 Britain leased the New Territories for 99 years - together defining Hong Kong's present-day extent.
* The 1941 Battle of Hong Kong: Japanese forces attacked Hong Kong in December 1941; after the Gin Drinker's Line - a defensive line running across the hills of the New Territories - fell, the garrison surrendered on "Black Christmas", December 25, beginning the "three years and eight months" of Japanese occupation. The trenches of the Shing Mun Redoubt along stage 6 of the MacLehose Trail remain clearly visible today.
* 1976 - the creation of the Country Parks: under Governor Murray MacLehose, the Country Parks Ordinance was enacted in 1976 and the first parks designated from the following year; today country parks and special areas cover about 40% of Hong Kong's land, a model of nature coexisting with a high-density city. The MacLehose Trail, opened in 1979, is named after him.
* The 1997 Handover: on July 1, Britain transferred sovereignty over Hong Kong to the People's Republic of China, and Hong Kong became a Special Administrative Region - where, under "One Country, Two Systems", the cityscape of Victoria Harbour and the wild trails of the country parks continue to coexist.
