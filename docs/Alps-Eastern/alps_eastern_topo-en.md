### Famous Peaks

This region spans Italy's South Tyrol and Trentino, Austria's Tyrol and Salzburg provinces, and extends into the northwestern corner of Slovenia, bringing together the limestone spires of the Dolomites and Austria's highest glaciated range:

* Grossglockner (3,798m): Austria's highest peak, the icy heart of Hohe Tauern National Park.
* Wildspitze (3,768m): the highest peak of Tyrol, rising at the center of the Ötztal Alps.
* Cevedale (3,769m): part of the same range as the Ortler, a signature summit of this large glaciated massif.
* Marmolada (3,343m): the highest peak of the Dolomites, home to a glacier that is a rarity in this range.
* Triglav (2,864m): Slovenia's highest peak and national symbol; its three-headed silhouette even appears on the country's flag.
* Dachstein (2,995m): at the northern edge of the map, the highest peak of Austria's Northern Limestone Alps.

### Famous Trekking Routes

* Alta Via 1:
  * Runs through the finest scenery of the Italian Dolomites, famous worldwide for its limestone spires, sheer cliffs, and comfortable mountain refuges (rifugi).
* Stubaier Höhenweg:
  * A high-altitude loop around the Stubai glacier group, linking numerous Austrian Alpine Club huts along the way.
* Adlerweg (Eagle Walk):
  * Its route traces the shape of an eagle in flight across the map, crossing the mountains of Tyrol as Austria's signature long-distance trail.

### Famous Sights

* Tre Cime di Lavaredo: on the eastern edge of the Dolomites, three towering rock pillars standing side by side form the region's most iconic landmark.
* Cortina d'Ampezzo: the fashionable resort capital of the Dolomites, past host of the Winter Olympics.
* Lake Garda & Arco: on the southern side of the map; beyond its lake-and-mountain scenery, Arco is also a world-class sport climbing destination.
* Grossglockner High Alpine Road: one of Europe's most spectacular mountain scenic roads, winding up to a glacier viewpoint.
* Triglav National Park & Lake Bled: Slovenia's only national park, famous for its island church and emerald-green lake.
* Innsbruck: the mountain-ringed capital of Tyrol, a key gateway into the Eastern Alps.

### Historical Events

* 1778 - First ascent of Triglav: a four-man team sponsored by Baron Sigmund Zois made the first ascent; climbing this peak is still regarded as a rite of passage for Slovenians, and its silhouette is featured on the national flag and coat of arms.
* 1800 - First ascent of Grossglockner: a scientific expedition organized by the Bishop of Gurk made the first ascent, a representative example of early Alpine climbing sponsored by the church and nobility.
* 1915-1917 - The "White War" (Guerra Bianca) in the Dolomites: during WWI, Italian and Austro-Hungarian troops fought fiercely amid the rock walls and glaciers thousands of meters up in the Dolomites, carving extensive trenches and tunnels; many of today's popular via ferratas, such as the Lagazuoi Tunnels, were converted from these wartime military passages.
* 1956 - Cortina d'Ampezzo Winter Olympics: Italy's first time hosting the Winter Olympics, propelling the Dolomites onto the international stage; in 2026, Cortina co-hosted the Winter Olympics again with Milan, returning to the world stage six decades later.
