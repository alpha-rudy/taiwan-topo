### Jeju Olle Trail

The Jeju Olle Trail (제주 올레길) is the main reason to use this map. "Olle" is a Jeju dialect word for the narrow lane leading from the main road to a family's front gate. In 2007, former journalist Suh Myung-sook, inspired by the Camino de Santiago in Spain, founded the Jeju Olle Foundation and opened Route 1 that September. It has since become one of Asia's best-known long-distance trails.

* The network
  * 27 routes totalling approx. 437km.
  * Main Routes 1 to 21 circle the island clockwise along the coast: from Siheung-ri in the east, along the south coast around Seogwipo, up the west coast, along the north coast through Jeju City, and back to Jongdal-ri in the northeast, near the starting point.
  * Island routes on Udo (1-1), Gapado (10-1) and Chujado (18-1), plus inland branch routes into the mid-mountain area.
  * Each route is approx. 10 to 20km, a walk of 4 to 7 hours. Most trailheads are served by bus, so the trail is easy to walk in stages.

* Recommended routes
  1. Route 1 (Siheung - Gwangchigi Beach): where the Olle begins, climbing Malmi Oreum and Al Oreum for views over Seongsan Ilchulbong and stone-walled fields.
  2. Route 1-1 (Udo): an approx. 11km loop around Udo Island, past Udobong peak and white coral-sand beaches.
  3. Route 6 (Soesokkak - Jeju Olle Tourist Center): along the Seogwipo coast, past Jeongbang Falls and through downtown Seogwipo.
  4. Route 7 (Jeju Olle Tourist Center - Wolpyeong): the Oedolgae sea stack and the coast west of Seogwipo; one of the most popular routes.
  5. Route 8 (Wolpyeong - Daepyeong): through the Jungmun resort area and the Daepo Jusangjeolli columnar-joint cliffs.
  6. Route 10 (Hwasun - Moseulpo): along the foot of Sanbangsan, the Yongmeori coast and Songaksan, past World War II Japanese airfield and cave remains.
  7. Route 10-1 (Gapado): a flat approx. 4km island walk, famous for its green barley fields in spring.
  8. Route 18-1 (Chujado): reached by ferry from Jeju Port; a hillier island route that is more physically demanding.

* Waymarks and the Olle Passport
  * The trail is marked by blue and orange ribbons tied to utility poles and branches, and by arrows painted on the ground and walls: blue arrows point forward (clockwise), orange arrows point in the reverse direction.
  * Pony-shaped "Ganse" markers stand at each route's start, midpoint and end, showing the route number and distances, with passport stamp stations.
  * Buy an Olle Passport to collect stamps at the start, midpoint and end of every route. Finishers of all routes can claim a completion certificate and medal at the Jeju Olle Tourist Center in Seogwipo.

* Tips for using this map
  * Most of the Olle follows ordinary roads, farm lanes, stone-walled paths and coastal tracks, all of which are drawn on this map. The contour lines and hillshading help you read the climbs over the oreum.
  * At present Routes 1 to 6 (including 3-A, 3-B and 6-A) and Route 14 are tagged completely enough in OSM to be highlighted as hiking routes on this map; the other routes are tagged inconsistently in OSM and are not yet highlighted. Use this map together with the route maps on the Jeju Olle website (https://www.jejuolle.org/) or the official app, or import GPX tracks.
  * Routes are sometimes rerouted for construction, conservation or private land. Always follow the ribbons and arrows on the ground.
  * Summers are hot and July to September is typhoon season, while winter brings strong northeasterly winds. The coastal sections have little shelter, so plan for sun, water and changing weather.

### Famous Peaks

1. Hallasan (center of Jeju Island)
    * Hallasan (1,947m) - the highest peak in South Korea, a national park, with the crater lake Baengnokdam at its summit
    * Saraoreum (1,324m) - a parasitic cone with a crater lake beside the Seongpanak trail
    * Witse Oreum (approx. 1,700m) - where the Eorimok and Yeongsil trails meet, with a shelter
    * Eoseungsaengak (1,169m) - a short summit hike next to the Eorimok trailhead

2. Parasitic cones, "Oreum" (some 360+ across the island)
    * Seongsan Ilchulbong (182m) - a tuff cone formed by an eruption in the sea, a World Natural Heritage site
    * Sanbangsan (395m) - a bell-shaped lava dome
    * Geomunoreum (456m) - source of the Manjanggul lava tube system, a World Natural Heritage site, guided tours by reservation
    * Darangshi Oreum (382m) - nicknamed the "queen of oreums", with a round crater about 115m deep
    * Saebyeol Oreum (519m) - venue of the Jeju Fire Festival every spring
    * Songaksan (104m) - a double volcano on the southwest coast

### Famous Trekking Routes

1. Hallasan Seongpanak Trail (Seongpanak - Jindallaebat Shelter - Baengnokdam summit, approx. 9.6km one way; the gentlest but longest)
2. Hallasan Gwaneumsa Trail (Gwaneumsa - Samgakbong Shelter - summit, approx. 8.7km one way; the most scenic and the steepest; a common traverse goes up Seongpanak and down Gwaneumsa)
    * Both summit trails require an online reservation in advance, and seasonal cut-off times apply for entry and turning back
3. Yeongsil and Eorimok Trails (to Witse Oreum and the Nambyeok junction; no summit access; known for the Yeongsil folding-screen cliffs and royal azaleas)
4. Jeju Olle Trail - a long-distance trail around the island's coast and villages, 27 routes totalling approx. 437km, walkable in stages
5. Hallasan Dulle-gil (the ring trail around Hallasan) and Saryeoni Forest Path (approx. 15km through cedar plantation and native forest)

### Famous Sights

* Seongsan Ilchulbong and Seopjikoji
* Manjanggul lava tube - part of the World Heritage Geomunoreum lava tube system, approx. 1km open to visitors
* Jusangjeolli columnar-joint cliffs at Daepo, Jungmun
* Cheonjiyeon, Jeongbang (one of the few waterfalls falling directly into the sea) and Cheonjeyeon waterfalls
* Udo (Udobong peak, coral-sand beaches) and Marado, the southernmost point of South Korea
* Yongduam (Dragon Head Rock), Hyeopjae Beach and Woljeongri Beach
* Bijarim Forest - a native forest of thousands of centuries-old nutmeg yews
* Haenyeo (women divers) culture and the Dol Hareubang stone statues

### Historical Events

* The Tamna Kingdom: Jeju was anciently known as "Tamna", which legend says was founded by three divine men, Go, Yang and Bu, who emerged from the Samseonghyeol holes. A semi-independent kingdom for centuries, it became a county of Goryeo in 1105 and was formally absorbed into the peninsula's dynasties.
* The 1273 Sambyeolcho resistance and Yuan rule: the Sambyeolcho army of Goryeo's military regime made its last stand against the Mongols at Hangpaduri Fortress on Jeju and was wiped out by a combined Goryeo-Mongol force. The Yuan then ran horse ranches on Jeju for about a century, founding the island's tradition of Jeju horses.
* The 1653 Hamel shipwreck: the Dutch East India Company ship Sperwer ran aground off Jeju's southwest coast. Crew member Hendrick Hamel was held in Joseon for thirteen years; the journal he wrote after escaping was the first detailed Western account of Korea. A memorial and a replica ship stand at the Yongmeori coast below Sanbangsan.
* The 1840-1848 exile of Kim Jeong-hui: the Joseon calligrapher Chusa Kim Jeong-hui was exiled to Daejeong on Jeju, where he perfected his "Chusa style" of calligraphy and painted his masterpiece "Sehando".
* The 1948 Jeju April 3 Incident: an armed uprising and a military and police crackdown that began on April 3, 1948 and lasted until 1954 killed tens of thousands of islanders, and remained a taboo subject for decades. It is commemorated today at the Jeju April 3rd Peace Park.
* The 2007 World Natural Heritage listing: "Jeju Volcanic Island and Lava Tubes" (Hallasan, Seongsan Ilchulbong and the Geomunoreum lava tube system) was inscribed as a UNESCO World Natural Heritage site; in 2016 the culture of the Jeju haenyeo was inscribed on the Intangible Cultural Heritage list.
