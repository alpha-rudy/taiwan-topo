Himalayas TOPO - Annapurna
==========================

## Map Coverage

* Latitude range: N28.0 ~ N29.0
* Longitude range: E83.0 ~ E85.0

### Famous Peaks

This region is home to numerous giants over 7,000m, several exceeding 8,000m:

* Annapurna I (8,091m): the world's 10th-highest peak, renowned for its extreme difficulty.
* Annapurna II, III, and IV: all magnificent peaks above 7,000m.
* Machhapuchchre (6,993m): famous for its perfect twin-pointed silhouette resembling a fish's tail; considered sacred and closed to climbing.
* Annapurna South (7,219m).
* Dhaulagiri I (8,167m): near the western edge of the coverage area (around E83.4°), the world's 7th-highest peak.
* Gangapurna (7,455m).
* Manaslu (8,163m): near the eastern edge of the coverage area (around E84.5°), the world's 8th-highest peak.

### Famous Trekking Routes

This region has Nepal's most well-developed and scenically diverse trekking routes:

* ACT - Annapurna Circuit Trek (230km)
  * Highlights: hailed as a world-class trek. It circles the entire Annapurna massif, crossing multiple climate zones from tropical rainforest to arid high plateau.
  * Key point: Thorong La Pass (5,416m), the highest point on the route.
* ABC - Annapurna Base Camp Trek (115km)
  * Highlights: goes deep into the Annapurna Sanctuary at the heart of the range, surrounded on all sides by peaks between 7,000 and 8,000m - utterly awe-inspiring.
* Poon Hill Trek (50km)
  * Highlights: a beginner-friendly route, usually starting from Ghorepani. Watching sunrise over the Dhaulagiri and Annapurna ranges from Poon Hill's summit is a classic sight.
* Mardi Himal Trek (55km)
  * Highlights: a recently popularized "hidden gem" route, passing very close to Machhapuchchre and offering superb close-up views of the snow peaks.
* Manaslu Circuit Trek (180km)
  * Highlights: on the eastern side of the coverage area, quieter and more remote than the ABC trek, crossing Larkya La Pass (5,106m).

### Famous Sights

* Pokhara: around N28.2°, E83.9°, the essential gateway city for all treks into the Annapurna region.
* Tilicho Lake (4,919m): one of the highest lakes in the world, reached via a side route off the Annapurna Circuit.
* Kali Gandaki Gorge: between Dhaulagiri and Annapurna, considered one of the deepest gorges in the world.
* Muktinath: a famous pilgrimage site, drawing countless Hindu and Buddhist devotees.

### Historical Events

* 1950 - First ascent of Annapurna I: Frenchmen Maurice Herzog and Louis Lachenal reached the summit, making it the first 8,000m peak ever climbed by humans, three years before Everest; both men lost multiple fingers and toes to severe frostbite, and Herzog's book "Annapurna" became a classic of mountaineering literature.
* 1956 - First ascent of Manaslu: a Japanese expedition led by Toshio Imanishi, with Sherpa Gyalzen Norbu, made the first ascent - considered one of the most important achievements in Japanese mountaineering history, and Manaslu is often called "Japan's mountain" as a result.
* 1960 - First ascent of Dhaulagiri I: a joint Swiss-Austrian expedition made the first ascent, and pioneered the use of a light aircraft to support high-altitude resupply, opening new possibilities for expedition logistics.
* 1957 - Machhapuchchre's near ascent: a British expedition turned back roughly 50m from the summit out of respect for local belief that the peak is sacred to Shiva; it remains formally unclimbed to this day.
