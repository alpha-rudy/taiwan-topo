Kiso Range - Chuo Alps
======================

AW3D30.OSM - Chuo-Alps TOPO v__version__

An offline map for Garmin / Android / iOS.

## Map Coverage

* Latitude range: N35.35 ~ N36.05
* Longitude range: E137.30 ~ E138.10

This map covers the whole of the Chuo Alps (Central Japanese Alps / Kiso Mountains), from Mount Kyo in the north to Mount Ena in the south, including the main ridge of Mount Kisokoma, Mount Hoken and Mount Utsugi, together with the free-standing volcano Mount Ontake to the west, the Kiso valley (the Kisoji section of the Nakasendo) and the Ina valley to the east. The main gateway towns are Komagane, Ina, Iida, Kiso-Fukushima and Nakatsugawa.

### Famous Trekking Routes

1. Senjojiki - Norikoshi-Jodo - Mount Kisokoma (the Komagatake Ropeway climbs straight to Senjojiki at 2,612m, the highest cable car station in Japan; the summit is a half-day walk)
2. Mount Kisokoma - Hinoo-dake - Mount Utsugi main ridge traverse (the classic two- to three-day route of the Central Alps)
3. Mount Utsugi via the Ikeyama ridge (from Komagane Kogen, over 1,700m of ascent on a long ridge)
4. Mount Kosumo - Minami-Koma-ga-take - Mount Utsugi circuit (from the Inagawa valley)
5. Mount Kisokoma by the Agematsu A and Fukushima B routes (the classic long direct climbs from the Kiso side)
6. Mount Ontake by the Kurosawa-guchi and Otaki-guchi pilgrim trails (ancient worship routes lined with huts and reijin stone monuments)
7. Mount Ena by the Hirogawara route or the old Maemiya pilgrim route
8. Mount Ampeiji and Surikogi-yama (a remote, rough traverse through deep bamboo grass)
9. Full traverse of the Central Alps (Mount Kyo - Kisokoma - Utsugi - Ampeiji - Ena)

### Famous Sights

* The Senjojiki Cirque (a glacial cirque and alpine flower garden reached directly by ropeway, right below Mount Hoken)
* The Komagatake Ropeway (a single 950m rise to the highest cable car station in Japan)
* Nezame-no-toko (granite gorge carved by the Kiso river, tied to the Urashima Taro legend)
* The Tsumago and Magome post towns on the Kisoji section of the Nakasendo (superbly preserved Edo-period townscapes)
* Akasawa Natural Recreational Forest (the birthplace of Japanese "forest bathing", with old-growth Kiso cypress and a forest railway)
* The Ontake worship landscape at Tanohara and Otaki, and the Kaida Highlands
* Hirugami Onsen, Komagane Kogen and Kozen-ji temple
* The Tenryu river, the river terraces of the Ina valley, and Bungui Pass

### Historical Events

* Kiso cypress and the Edo-period "closed mountain" system: the forests of the Kiso valley were controlled directly by the Owari domain, and unauthorised felling was punished under the rule of "one tree, one head" - one of the earliest forest protection systems in Japan. Kiso cypress is still used for the periodic rebuilding of the Ise Grand Shrine.
* The eleven Kiso post towns of the Nakasendo: the inland highway between Edo and Kyoto ran through the Kiso valley, and the preservation movement begun in Tsumago and Magome in the 1960s pioneered historic townscape conservation in Japan.
* Ontake worship and its pilgrim trails: long a Shugendo holy mountain, Ontake was opened to ordinary pilgrims in the Edo period by the ascetics Kakumei and Fukan, giving rise to Ontake-ko confraternities all over Japan; hundreds of reijin monuments still stand on the mountain.
* 1913: the Mount Kisokoma school disaster, in which a group climb by Nakaminowa Higher Elementary School was caught in a storm and 11 people died. It was later told in Jiro Nitta's novel *Seishoku no Ishibumi*, and a memorial stands near the summit.
* 1967: the opening of the Komagatake Ropeway made the Central Alps one of the most accessible 3,000m-class ranges in Japan.
* 1984: the Naganoken-Seibu earthquake (M6.8), centred on Otaki village, triggered the massive "Ontake collapse" on the southern flank of the volcano, killing or leaving missing 29 people.
* 27 September 2014: the phreatic eruption of Mount Ontake struck at the height of the autumn climbing season, killing 58 people with 5 missing - Japan's deadliest volcanic disaster since the war - and led to a major strengthening of volcano monitoring and evacuation systems nationwide.
