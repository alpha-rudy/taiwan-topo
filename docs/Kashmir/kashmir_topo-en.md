### Famous Peaks

This region lies between the Pir Panjal Range and the Great Himalaya; although not as high as the Nepal side of the Himalaya, the terrain is rugged and snow-capped year-round:
* Kolahoi Peak (5,425m): in the southeast of the coverage area (around E75.3°), nicknamed the "Matterhorn of Kashmir"; a well-known pyramidal technical peak and the source of the Kolahoi Glacier.
* Mount Haramukh (5,142m): in the north of the coverage area, a peak held deeply sacred by local Hindus; legend has it that the height of K2 was first measured by triangulation from this summit.
* Mahadev Peak (3,966m): rising behind Srinagar; though not especially high, it is the highest point of the Zabarwan Range and is clearly visible from the city.
* Apharwat Peak (4,390m): on the western edge of the coverage area near Gulmarg; a famous ski destination served by one of the world's highest cable cars.

### Famous Trekking Routes

This region has some of the Indian Himalaya's most classic alpine-lake treks, known for lush high mountain meadows and sapphire-blue lakes:
* KGL - Kashmir Great Lakes Trek (72km)
  * Highlights: widely regarded as one of India's most beautiful treks. The route runs mainly between N34.2 and N34.4, crossing several high-altitude passes and passing stunning alpine lakes such as Vishansar, Kishansar, and Gadsar.
  * Key point: Gadsar Pass (4,200m), the highest point on the route, also known as the "Pass of Death."
* Tarsar Marsar Trek (48km)
  * Highlights: starts from the Aru Valley in the south of the coverage area. This route leads hikers to a pair of twin lakes cradled by mountains, picture-perfect scenery, and a more relaxed pace than the KGL trek.
* Naranag - Gangabal Trek (30km)
  * Highlights: at the foot of Mount Haramukh, a pilgrimage route with a long history. Gangabal Lake is one of the largest alpine lakes in the area and is regarded as one of the sources of the Ganges.
* Kolahoi Glacier Trek (35km)
  * Highlights: follows the Lidder Valley upstream to the terminus of the Kolahoi Glacier's ice tongue, offering a close-up view of glacial landforms.

### Famous Sights

* Srinagar: at N34.08°, E74.8°, Kashmir's summer capital, known as the "Venice of the East."
* Dal Lake: Srinagar's landmark, famous for its houseboats and shikara boats, its waters reflecting the surrounding mountains.
* Sonamarg: at N34.3°, E75.2°, meaning "meadow of gold." The starting point of the KGL trek and a key gateway to Ladakh (near Zoji La Pass).
* Pahalgam: on the southern edge of the coverage area (around N34.01°), the famous "Valley of Shepherds" and a key base for pilgrimage to the Amarnath cave shrine.
* Wular Lake: in the northwest of the coverage area, one of India's largest freshwater lakes, crucial for regulating water levels across the Kashmir Valley.
* Mughal Gardens: such as Nishat Bagh and Shalimar Bagh, scattered along the shores of Dal Lake, showcasing the finest of Persian-style garden architecture.

### Historical Events

* 17th century - Construction of the Mughal gardens: Mughal Emperors Jahangir and Shah Jahan built Shalimar Bagh (1619) and Nishat Bagh (1633) in succession on the shores of Dal Lake, ushering in the golden age of Kashmir's Persian-style gardens.
* The millennia-old trade route over Zoji La: this ancient road connecting the Kashmir Valley to Ladakh and onward to Central Asia has long served as an important branch of the Silk Road, carrying a steady stream of caravans and pilgrims.
* Early 20th century - Exploration by the Neve brothers (Arthur & Ernest Neve): these British missionary doctors practiced medicine in Srinagar for decades while also pioneering the exploration of Kashmir's high mountains; much of the early route documentation for peaks such as Kolahoi comes from their work.
* 1846 - The Treaty of Amritsar: the British East India Company transferred the Kashmir Valley to Maharaja Gulab Singh of Jammu, establishing the territorial foundation of the princely state of Jammu and Kashmir.
