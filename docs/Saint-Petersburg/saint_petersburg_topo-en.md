### Famous Walking Routes

St. Petersburg is built across more than forty islands in the Neva River delta, its canals and bridges linking Baroque palaces to Neoclassical streetscapes - the most walkable city in Russia; during the White Nights of summer, strolling the embankments at midnight to watch the bridges raise is a truly unique experience.

* Nevsky Prospekt:
  * St. Petersburg's central axis, running about 4.5km from the Admiralty building straight to Alexander Nevsky Monastery. Along the way it passes Kazan Cathedral, the House of Books, and Yeliseyev Emporium - the first route for getting a feel for the city's rhythm.
* Palace Square - Palace Embankment:
  * Starting from Palace Square beneath the Alexander Column, stroll along the granite embankment on the south bank of the Neva, with the golden spire of the Peter and Paul Fortress across the water; during White Nights, this is the classic spot to watch the Palace Bridge raise at midnight.
* Griboyedov Canal:
  * From the Church of the Savior on Spilled Blood, follow the canal south past the gilded griffins of Bank Bridge to behind Kazan Cathedral, then continue on to the Mariinsky Theatre area - the best walk for soaking up the "Venice of the North" atmosphere.
* Peter and Paul Fortress - Zayachy Island:
  * Circling the fortress walls offers a panorama of the Winter Palace embankment; in summer people even sunbathe on the small beach beneath the walls, and the noon signal cannon is a century-old tradition.
* Strelka - Universitetskaya Embankment:
  * Starting from the Strelka square with its red Rostral Columns, follow the University Embankment past the Kunstkamera, the Twelve Collegia, and on to Menshikov Palace, combining Neva River views with an academic atmosphere.
* Summer Garden - Field of Mars:
  * A French-style garden laid out by Peter the Great himself, its tree-lined paths set off by marble statues and fountains; just beyond the garden lies the open Field of Mars and the Mikhailovsky Castle, ideal for an unhurried afternoon.
* New Holland Island:
  * A triangular artificial island once used for 18th-century shipbuilding timber warehouses, now converted into lawns, bookshops, and eateries - a favorite spot for locals' weekend picnics and strolls.
* Yelagin Island / TsPKiO:
  * A car-free green island in the northwest of the city, with an approximately 6km loop trail; golden autumn foliage reflects beautifully on the ponds, and Yelagin Palace on the island is a Neoclassical masterpiece.
* Tsarskoye Selo (Pushkin) - Catherine Park:
  * An imperial park on the southern edge of the map, centered on Catherine Palace, with French formal gardens and English-style lakeside paths stretching for kilometers; combined with a palace visit, it can fill most of a day.

### Famous Sights

* State Hermitage Museum / Winter Palace:
  * One of the world's four great museums; the Malachite Room, the Jordan Staircase, and some three million items in the collection all sit within this blue, white, and gold Baroque palace - set aside a full day.
* Church of the Savior on Spilled Blood:
  * Built to commemorate the assassination of Alexander II, its colorful onion domes echo Moscow's St. Basil's Cathedral; the interior holds over 7,000 square meters of mosaic work unmatched anywhere in the world.
* St. Isaac's Cathedral:
  * One of the largest domed cathedrals in the world, its gilded dome rising 101.5m; climb 262 spiral steps to the colonnade viewing platform for a 360-degree view of the city skyline.
* Peter and Paul Fortress:
  * The founding site of St. Petersburg in 1703; its cathedral holds the tombs of the Romanov tsars from Peter the Great onward.
* Kazan Cathedral:
  * Modeled on St. Peter's Basilica in Rome, its 96-column curved colonnade embraces Nevsky Prospekt; a memorial to the 1812 Patriotic War, and the resting place of Field Marshal Kutuzov.
* State Russian Museum:
  * Housed in the Mikhailovsky Palace, holding the finest collection of native Russian art, considered the counterpart to Moscow's Tretyakov Gallery.
* Mariinsky Theatre:
  * The great house of Russian ballet and opera, where "Swan Lake" and "The Nutcracker" both premiered; the pale-green historic theater is itself a sight to see.
* Smolny Convent:
  * A blue-and-white Baroque complex designed by Rastrelli, celebrated as the most elegant church silhouette in St. Petersburg.
* Catherine Palace & Amber Room (Tsarskoye Selo):
  * In the town of Pushkin on the southern edge of the map, this sky-blue Baroque palace stretches over 300m; the reconstructed Amber Room is dazzling, making it the most popular day trip from St. Petersburg.
* Lakhta Center:
  * At 462m, Europe's tallest building, its spiraling glass tower rising beside the Gulf of Finland in striking contrast to the historic city skyline.

### Historical Events

* 1703 - Founding of St. Petersburg: Peter the Great built this "window on Europe" starting from the Peter and Paul Fortress on the marshy delta of the Neva; it formally became the capital of the Russian Empire in 1712.
* 1825 - The Decembrist Revolt: a group of noble officers staged an uprising in Senate Square (now Decembrists' Square), demanding constitutional reform; though suppressed, it became the opening act of Russia's modern revolutionary movement.
* 1905 - "Bloody Sunday": troops opened fire on petitioners in front of the Winter Palace, igniting the Revolution of 1905 and sowing the seeds for the eventual overthrow of the tsarist system.
* 1917 - The October Revolution: Bolshevik forces stormed the Winter Palace, with the cruiser Aurora firing a blank shot as the signal for the uprising, and the Soviet government was born; the city's name would later change to Petrograd (1914) and Leningrad (1924), before reverting to St. Petersburg after the Soviet collapse in 1991.
* 1941-1944 - The Siege of Leningrad: Nazi German forces besieged the city for 872 days, during which over a million residents died of starvation, cold, and bombardment - one of the most devastating sieges in human history, and a defining symbol of the Soviet fight in WWII.
