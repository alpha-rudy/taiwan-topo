### Famous Peaks

This region covers the core of the Western Alps, spanning eastern France, the Swiss canton of Valais, and the Aosta Valley in northwestern Italy, extending south to the Maritime Alps, and bringing together some of the Alps' most iconic 4,000m giants:

* Mont Blanc (4,810m): the highest peak in the Alps, on the France-Italy border, its vast glaciers world-renowned.
* Matterhorn / Monte Cervino (4,478m): on the Switzerland-Italy border, its solitary pyramidal form the spiritual symbol of the Alps.
* Monte Rosa - Dufourspitze (4,634m): Switzerland's highest peak, a massive massif with numerous subsidiary summits above 4,000m.
* Dom (4,545m): the main peak of the Mischabel range, the highest summit entirely within Swiss territory.
* Gran Paradiso (4,061m): the highest peak lying entirely within Italy; its namesake national park is a haven for Alpine ibex.
* Barre des Écrins (4,102m): on the western side of the map, the southernmost 4,000m peak in France.
* Monviso (3,841m): the source of Italy's Po River, its pyramidal form the most conspicuous landmark on the Piedmont plain.

### Famous Trekking Routes

* Tour du Mont Blanc (TMB):
  * About 170km long, crossing France, Italy, and Switzerland in a full circuit of Mont Blanc; one of the world's most popular classic long-distance treks.
* Walker's Haute Route:
  * Links the two great mountaineering meccas of Chamonix and Zermatt, weaving between high glaciers and classic Alpine villages.
* GR5 (French section):
  * Runs south from Lake Geneva through Vanoise and Mercantour National Parks all the way to the Mediterranean.

### Famous Sights

* Chamonix-Mont-Blanc: France's outdoor and mountaineering capital at the foot of Mont Blanc; a cable car runs straight up to Aiguille du Midi for sweeping glacier views.
* Zermatt: Switzerland's famous car-free mountain resort, the prime base for viewing the Matterhorn's reflection and for high-mountain hiking.
* Courmayeur: an elegant resort town on Mont Blanc's Italian side, linked to Chamonix by the Mont Blanc Tunnel.
* Vanoise & Gran Paradiso National Parks: straddling the France-Italy border, among the Alps' oldest and most ecologically rich protected high-mountain areas.
* Mercantour National Park: on the southern edge of the map, known for its wilderness trails and the prehistoric rock engravings of the "Valley of Wonders."

### Historical Events

* 1786 - First ascent of Mont Blanc: Michel-Gabriel Paccard and guide Jacques Balmat made the first ascent, widely regarded as the founding moment of modern mountaineering (alpinism); scientist Horace-Bénédict de Saussure had offered a reward for the first ascent, and personally made the second ascent the following year, in 1787.
* 1865 - First ascent of the Matterhorn: Edward Whymper led the team that made the first ascent, but on the descent the rope broke, sending four climbers to their deaths in a major mountaineering disaster; the tragedy also marked the symbolic end of the famous "Golden Age of Alpinism" (1854-1865).
* 1856 - Gran Paradiso designated a royal hunting reserve: established by King Victor Emmanuel II of Italy to protect the endangered Alpine ibex, it became Italy's first national park in 1922.
* 1924 - The first Winter Olympics held in Chamonix: cementing Chamonix's international status as one of the birthplaces of modern mountaineering and skiing.
