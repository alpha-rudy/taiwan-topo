### Famous Walking Routes

Moscow is a wonderfully walkable city: the center radiates outward from the Kremlin in concentric rings, with riverside promenades, tree-lined boulevards, and large forest parks weaving together scenery that changes with every season.

* Red Square - Zaryadye Park - Moskva River Embankment:
  * A classic central walking axis. Start at Red Square, cross Zaryadye Park's "Floating Bridge" for a panoramic view of the Kremlin, then stroll along the Moskva riverside promenade - about 3-5km, the top choice for a first visit to Moscow.
* Old Arbat:
  * Moscow's most famous pedestrian street, about 1.2km long, lined with street performers, bookshops, and cafes; connects to the Pushkin House Museum for a taste of 19th-century literary Moscow.
* Gorky Park - Muzeon - Krymskaya Embankment:
  * The most popular green corridor on the south bank of the Moskva, with outdoor seating and bike rentals in summer and a huge ice rink in winter; Muzeon displays Soviet-era statues, and the paths are flat and easy walking.
* Sparrow Hills - Moscow State University:
  * Climb to the Sparrow Hills viewpoint for views over the great bend of the Moskva River and Luzhniki Stadium, then walk around the main building of Moscow State University, the tallest of Stalin's "Seven Sisters," before descending to the riverbank on a forest trail.
* Kolomenskoye:
  * A former royal estate and park on the Moskva River, home to the UNESCO World Heritage Church of the Ascension. Its grounds are extensive and gently rolling, ideal for a leisurely half-day stroll, especially lovely when the apple orchards bloom in spring.
* Tsaritsyno:
  * Catherine the Great's Gothic Revival palace and its expansive landscaped park, with lakeside promenades and woodland paths linking together the most elegant walking grounds in the south of the city.
* Losiny Ostrov National Park:
  * A large old-growth forest in the northeast of the city, billed as "taiga within the city"; a well-developed trail network where, with luck, you might spot wild elk - ideal for anyone wanting a proper hike within an urban setting.

### Famous Sights

* Moscow Kremlin & Red Square:
  * The political and historical heart of Russia, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Within its walls stand the Assumption Cathedral, the Ivan the Great Bell Tower, the Tsar Bell, and the Tsar Cannon - allow at least half a day to see it properly.
* St. Basil's Cathedral:
  * The multicolored onion-domed cathedral at the south end of Red Square, built in 1561 to commemorate the capture of Kazan; Moscow's most iconic landmark.
* Cathedral of Christ the Saviour:
  * One of the tallest Orthodox churches in the world, its white walls and gold dome standing beside the Moskva River; the Patriarchal Bridge offers a classic photo of the river with the Kremlin in view.
* Novodevichy Convent:
  * A UNESCO World Heritage Site and the finest example of 17th-century "Moscow Baroque" architecture; the adjoining cemetery is the resting place of Chekhov, Gogol, and many other notable figures.
* Tretyakov Gallery:
  * The foremost repository of Russian art, spanning everything from ancient icons to masterpieces of the Peredvizhniki movement; essential for understanding Russian art history.
* VDNKh (All-Russia Exhibition Centre):
  * A grand Soviet-era exhibition park with sweeping fountains and pavilions, near the Ostankino TV Tower and the Cosmonautics Museum; worth a full day.
* Moscow Metro:
  * Known as the "underground palace"; stations such as Komsomolskaya and Mayakovskaya are adorned with marble, mosaics, and chandeliers - a museum you can ride through.
* Izmailovo Kremlin:
  * A fairy-tale-style wooden building complex next to Moscow's largest crafts flea market; a great place to shop for matryoshka dolls and other souvenirs.

### Historical Events

* 1147 - Founding of Moscow: the city's first mention in historical records, as a fortified settlement built by Grand Prince Yuri Dolgoruky - the precursor to today's Kremlin.
* 1237-1240 - The Mongol invasion of the Golden Horde: Moscow was burned by Mongol forces and remained subject to Golden Horde rule for over two centuries, until Ivan III repelled the Mongols in 1480 and the Grand Duchy of Moscow gained full independence.
* 1712 - The capital moves to St. Petersburg: Peter the Great relocated the capital to his newly built city of St. Petersburg, and Moscow's political standing waned until the Soviet government moved the capital back to Moscow in 1918.
* 1812 - Napoleon's invasion and the Great Fire of Moscow: shortly after French forces occupied Moscow, a mysterious fire broke out and destroyed much of the city; Napoleon was forced to retreat in the depths of winter, a turning point that marked the decline of his empire.
* 1941-1942 - The Battle of Moscow: during WWII, German forces advanced to within a few dozen kilometers of the city - the furthest point reached on the Eastern Front - before the Soviet Red Army launched a counteroffensive that turned the tide of the war.
