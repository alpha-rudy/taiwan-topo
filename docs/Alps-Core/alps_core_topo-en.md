### Famous Peaks

This region is centered on Switzerland, extending east to include the canton of Graubünden and south to the lake district of northern Italy, linking the magnificent glaciated peaks from the Bernese Oberland to the Engadin:

* Jungfrau (4,158m): together with the Eiger and Mönch, forms the most spectacular trio of peaks in the Bernese Oberland.
* Finsteraarhorn (4,274m): the highest peak of the Bernese Oberland, rising at the heart of the Aletsch glacier system.
* Piz Bernina (4,049m): the easternmost 4,000m peak in the entire Alps, dominating the Engadin.
* Ortler / Ortles (3,905m): once the highest peak of the Austro-Hungarian Empire, towering over the western side of South Tyrol.
* Tödi (3,614m): the highest peak of the Glarus Alps, a massive, snow-covered summit.
* Säntis (2,502m): in the Appenzell region at the northern edge of the map; on a clear day, views extend across six countries.

### Famous Trekking Routes

* Alpine Pass Route:
  * Crosses Switzerland from east to west over more than a dozen traditional mountain passes, a classic way to experience the country's mountain landscapes from end to end.
* Eiger Trail:
  * Passes directly beneath the breathtaking Eiger North Face, offering close-up views of mountaineering's most legendary wall.
* Senda Engiadina:
  * Follows the sunlit slopes above the Engadin valley, linking a string of traditional Romansh villages with their distinctive sgraffito facades.

### Famous Sights

* Grindelwald: a storybook village in the Jungfrau region, the classic gateway for pilgrimages to the Eiger North Face.
* Lauterbrunnen: Switzerland's famous "waterfall village," set in a deep U-shaped glacial valley with 72 spectacular falls.
* Jungfraujoch: known as the "Top of Europe," Europe's highest railway station, overlooking the source of the Aletsch Glacier.
* Aletsch Glacier: the longest glacier in the Alps, inscribed as a UNESCO World Natural Heritage Site.
* St. Moritz: a glittering resort town in the Engadin valley, twice host of the Winter Olympics.
* Lake Como & Lugano: the northern Italian lake district on the southern edge of the map, where mountains plunge straight into deep blue lakes for a quintessentially Italian resort atmosphere.

### Historical Events

* 1811 - First ascent of the Jungfrau: the Meyer brothers made the first successful summit, a landmark moment in the mountaineering history of the Bernese Oberland.
* 1858 - First ascent of the Eiger: made by Charles Barrington with guides Christian Almer and Peter Bohren.
* 1938 - First ascent of the Eiger North Face: a German-Austrian team of four, led by Anderl Heckmair, conquered the face long known as the "Mordwand" (Murder Wall); two years earlier, in 1936, Toni Kurz died attempting the same wall in a tragedy watched in full through telescopes by guests at the Kleine Scheidegg hotel below - one of the most famous accidents in mountaineering history.
* 1850 - First ascent of Piz Bernina: made by Swiss surveyor Johann Coaz, establishing the peak as the easternmost 4,000m summit in the entire Alps.
