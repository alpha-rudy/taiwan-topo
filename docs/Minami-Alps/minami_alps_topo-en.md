Southern Ridge - Minami Alps
============================

AW3D30.OSM - Minami-Alps TOPO v__version__

An offline map for Garmin / Android / iOS.

## Map Coverage

* Latitude range: N35.15 ~ N35.90
* Longitude range: E137.70 ~ E138.65

This map covers the whole of the Minami Alps (Southern Japanese Alps / Akaishi Mountains), from Mount Kaikoma in the north to Mount Tekari in the south, including the Shirane Sanzan, the Hoo Sanzan and the Shiomi-Warusawa-Akaishi-Hijiri main ridge, with the western rim of the Kofu basin and the Minobu hills to the east and the southern Ina valley and Iida to the west. The main gateways are Kofu and Nirasaki (Hirogawara, Yashajin Pass), Ina (Kitazawa Pass), Iida (Yorodo, Torikura) and Hatanagi / Ikawa on the Shizuoka side.

### Famous Peaks

1. The North: Kaikoma, Senjo and Hoo
    * Mount Kaikoma (2,967m, 100 Famous Japanese Mountains, a white granite pyramid; its Kurodo ridge is one of Japan's three steepest ascents)
    * Mount Senjo (3,033m, 100 Famous Japanese Mountains, the "Queen of the Southern Alps" with three glacial cirques)
    * The Hoo Sanzan (100 Famous Japanese Mountains): Jizo-dake (2,764m, with the famous granite Obelisk), Kannon-dake (2,841m), Yakushi-dake (2,780m)
    * Asayo-mine (2,799m, on the Hayakawa ridge)

2. The Shirane Sanzan (northern main ridge)
    * Mount Kita (3,193m, the second highest peak in Japan, 100 Famous Japanese Mountains, home of the endemic Kitadake-so flower and the Okanbazawa snow gully)
    * Mount Aino (3,190m, joint third highest in Japan, 100 Famous Japanese Mountains)
    * Mount Notori (3,026m, the southern end of the Shirane Sanzan)

3. The Middle: Shiomi, Arakawa and Akaishi
    * Mount Shiomi (3,047m, 100 Famous Japanese Mountains, a black twin-horned "iron helmet")
    * Mount Warusawa (Arakawa-Higashi-dake, 3,141m, sixth highest in Japan, 100 Famous Japanese Mountains)
    * Mount Akaishi (3,121m, 100 Famous Japanese Mountains, namesake of the Akaishi range, with the highest first-order triangulation point in Japan)

4. The South
    * Mount Hijiri (3,013m, the southernmost 3,000m peak in Japan, 100 Famous Japanese Mountains)
    * Kamikochi-dake (2,803m) and Chausu-dake (2,604m)
    * Mount Tekari (2,592m, 100 Famous Japanese Mountains, the southern limit of naturally growing creeping pine)
    * Mount Zaru (2,629m, 200 Famous Japanese Mountains, the viewpoint peak of the Shirane southern ridge)

### Famous Trekking Routes

1. Shirane Sanzan traverse (Hirogawara - Mount Kita - Mount Aino - Mount Notori - Narada, among the highest ridge walks in Japan)
2. Mount Kaikoma and Mount Senjo (day climbs from Kitazawa Pass; the Kurodo ridge on Kaikoma gains 2,200m, one of Japan's three steepest ascents)
3. Hoo Sanzan traverse (Yashajin Pass - Yakushi - Kannon - Jizo - Gozaishi or Aoki mineral springs)
4. Mount Shiomi (Torikura trailhead - Sanpuku Pass - Mount Shiomi, over one of the highest passes in Japan)
5. Arakawa Sanzan - Mount Akaishi circuit (from Sawarajima: Senmai-dake - Warusawa - Arakawa-Naka-dake - Akaishi, with the Tokai Forest mountain bus)
6. Mount Hijiri to Mount Tekari traverse (from Yorodo / Tashiro, via Hijiridaira hut, Kamikochi-dake, Chausu-dake and Tekari hut)
7. The Senshio ridge (the long, remote ridge between Mount Senjo and Mount Shiomi, the "backbone of the Southern Alps")
8. Full traverse of the Southern Alps (Kaikoma to Tekari, 7-10 days, among the longest 3,000m ridge walks in Japan)

### Famous Sights

* The Southern Alps forest-road bus (Senryuso - Kitazawa Pass) and the Todai riverbed
* Hirogawara and Yashajin Pass (the Kofu-side gateway) and Ashiyasu Onsen
* Sawarajima and the Hatanagi No.1 Dam (the Shizuoka-side gateway; the Hatanagi suspension bridge is among the highest in Japan)
* The Ikawa line of the Oigawa Railway (Japan's only rack-and-pinion mountain railway), Lake Ikawa and Sessokyo Onsen
* Sumatakyo Onsen and the "Bridge of Dreams"
* Kuon-ji temple on Mount Minobu (head temple of Nichiren Buddhism, with a 400-year-old weeping cherry)
* Shimoguri-no-sato (the "Japanese Tyrol", a sky-high hamlet in Toyama-go)
* The Median Tectonic Line outcrops at Oshika village and Kashio Onsen (a rare inland salt spring)

### Historical Events

* Edo-period worship climbing: Mount Kaikoma was opened in 1816 by the ascetic Kohan (Obi Gonzaburo), and the Kurodo ridge is still lined with stone shrines and reijin monuments; the Sai-no-kawara below Jizo-dake on Hoo has long been a place of prayer for children.
* In 1892 the British missionary Walter Weston climbed Mount Kaikoma, Hoo and other peaks and introduced the Southern Alps to the world; in 1905 he and Kojima Usui co-founded the Japanese Alpine Club, and the modern exploration of these mountains began in earnest.
* 1957: completion of the Ikawa Dam. The Ikawa railway line, laid for its construction, later became a scenic railway and a key approach to the deep southern Alps.
* 1964: designation of Minami Alps National Park, spanning the main ridge across Yamanashi, Nagano and Shizuoka.
* 2014: the Southern Alps were registered as a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve (Eco Park), recognising the coexistence of the 3,000m ridgeline and the mountain-foot communities.
* The Chuo Shinkansen (maglev) Southern Alps tunnel, bored directly beneath the range since the 2010s, has caused a long-running dispute with Shizuoka Prefecture over reduced flow in the Oi river - one of Japan's major environmental controversies.
