Shrine & Marshland - Nikko-Oze
==============================

AW3D30.OSM - Nikko-Oze TOPO v__version__

An offline map for Garmin / Android / iOS.

## Map Coverage

  * Latitude range: N36.50 ~ N37.47
  * Longitude range: E138.68 ~ E139.86

### Famous Peaks

1. Nikko area (mainly Tochigi Prefecture)
    * Mount Nantai (2,486m) - a Hyakumeizan; its summit hosts the inner shrine of Futarasan Shrine and is the spiritual center of Nikko's faith
    * Mount Nyoho (2,483m) - together with Nantai and Taro, forms the "Three Mountains of Nikko"
    * Mount Taro (2,368m)
    * Mount Nikko-Shirane / Oku-Shirane (2,578m) - a Hyakumeizan and the highest peak in the Kanto region
    * Nasu Mountain Range / Mount Chausu (1,915m) - a Hyakumeizan and an active volcano

2. Oze area (border of Gunma, Fukushima, and Niigata)
    * Mount Hiuchigatake (2,356m) - a Hyakumeizan and the highest peak in the Tohoku region
    * Mount Shibutsu (2,228m) - a Hyakumeizan; its serpentine rock supports a distinctive alpine plant community
    * Aizu-Komagatake (2,133m) - a Hyakumeizan
    * Mount Akagi (1,827m) - a Hyakumeizan; a composite volcano with a scenic crater lake, Lake Onuma, at its summit
    * Mount Sukai (2,144m) - a Hyakumeizan

### Famous Trekking Routes

1. Nikko area (mainly Tochigi Prefecture)
    * Senjogahara Trail: a boardwalk across the highland marsh stretching from the shore of Lake Chuzenji to Yumoto, with distinct scenery in every season
    * Yudaki Falls - Yumoto Onsen Trail: a short trail along Yudaki Falls to Lake Yu and Yumoto Onsen
    * Kirifuri Highland - Komaruyama Trail: offers views of the Nikko mountain range, famous for autumn foliage and views from the Roppouzawa Bridge
    * Mount Nantai pilgrimage trail: starts from the Chugushi shrine of Futarasan Shrine, a full-day round trip and the traditional pilgrimage route of Nikko's faith
    * Mount Nikko-Shirane cable car trail: ride the cable car to the summit station, then hike to the top and back - the most accessible route to Kanto's highest peak

2. Oze area (border of Gunma, Fukushima, and Niigata)
    * Ozegahara Traverse: a boardwalk crossing the heart of the Oze marshland, most popular during the mizubasho (skunk cabbage) bloom in June and the Oze gentian season in August
    * Ozenuma Lake Loop: circles Lake Ozenuma, with views of Mount Hiuchigatake reflected in the water
    * Mihari - Onsen Hut Cluster Trail: a mountain hut settlement on the east side of Ozegahara, an important waypoint on longer traverses
    * Mount Hiuchigatake summit route (starting from Oike or Numayama Pass, round trip via Lake Ozenuma)
    * Mount Shibutsu summit route (starting from Hatomachi Pass, with a summit panorama over all of Ozegahara)

### Famous Sights

* Nikko Toshogu: the mausoleum of Tokugawa Ieyasu; inscribed together with Futarasan Shrine and Rinno-ji Temple as the UNESCO World Heritage "Shrines and Temples of Nikko" in 1999
* Lake Chuzenji: a high-altitude lake dammed by Mount Nantai's volcanic activity, its shore lined with hot-spring inns
* Kegon Falls: a 97m drop, one of Japan's three great waterfalls, and the sole outlet of Lake Chuzenji
* Ryuzu Falls and Yudaki Falls: well-known waterfalls of Oku-Nikko, especially spectacular amid autumn foliage
* Oku-Nikko Yumoto Onsen: the innermost hot-spring town of Nikko, right beside Lake Yu and Yudaki Falls
* Kinugawa Onsen: one of Tochigi Prefecture's signature hot-spring towns, close to central Nikko
* Ozegahara Marshland: Japan's largest high-altitude wetland, the birthplace of a 1960s conservation movement, now a Ramsar-listed wetland
* Nasu Onsen towns: a cluster of hot springs at the foot of the Nasu Mountain Range, home to the Imperial Family's Nasu Villa

### Historical Events

* 782 - The founding of Nikko: tradition holds that the monk Shodo Shonin made the first ascent of Mount Nantai and founded Nikko's religious tradition, later building temples at the mountain's foot that laid the groundwork for Nikko as a sacred site.
* 1617 - Founding of Nikko Toshogu: built by the Tokugawa shogunate to enshrine its founder, Tokugawa Ieyasu, as the deity Tosho Daigongen; Nikko subsequently became the shogunate's most important site of religious and political symbolism, with successive shoguns making regular pilgrimages there (Nikko Shasan).
* 1949 - The song "Natsu no Omoide" (Summer Memories) is released: this famous song depicting Oze's sea of mizubasho flowers was sung across Japan, turning the once-remote Oze marshland into a national holiday destination and planting the seed for the later conservation movement.
* 1949-1996 - The Oze conservation movement: the government's original plans to build a road and dam across Ozegahara were met with sustained grassroots opposition; the plans were eventually withdrawn entirely, making Oze a pioneering case in Japan's nature conservation history.
