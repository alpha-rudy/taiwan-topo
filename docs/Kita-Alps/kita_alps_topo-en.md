Roof of Japan - Kita Alps
=========================

AW3D30.OSM - Kita-Alps TOPO v__version__

An offline map for Garmin / Android / iOS.

## Map Coverage

* Latitude range: N35.90 ~ N37.05
* Longitude range: E137.15 ~ E138.25

This map covers the entire Kita Alps (Northern Japanese Alps / Hida Mountains) across Nagano, Toyama, Gifu and Niigata prefectures, from the Sea of Japan coast at Oyashirazu in the north to Mount Norikura in the south, including Tateyama-Kurobe, the Ushiro-Tateyama ridge, the Yari-Hotaka range and Kamikochi, together with the main gateway towns of Matsumoto, Omachi, Toyama and Takayama.

### Famous Trekking Routes

1. Omote-Ginza traverse (Nakabusa Onsen - Tsubakuro-dake - Otensho-dake - Nishi-dake - Higashi-Kama ridge - Mount Yari)
2. Ura-Ginza traverse (Takase Dam - Eboshi-dake - Noguchi-Goro-dake - Mount Suisho - Mount Washiba - Sugoroku-dake - Mount Yari)
3. Kamikochi - Yokoo - Karasawa Cirque - Mount Oku-Hotaka (the autumn colours of Karasawa are among Japan's most famous mountain scenes)
4. The Dai-Kiretto traverse (Kita-Hotaka - Dai-Kiretto - Minami-dake, one of Japan's classic knife-edge ridges)
5. Nishi-Hotaka to Oku-Hotaka traverse (including Uma-no-se, one of the hardest non-technical traverses in Japan)
6. Mount Tsurugi via the Bessan or Hayatsuki ridge (the Kani-no-Tatebai / Yokobai chained sections)
7. Shirouma Daisekkei - Mount Shirouma - the Three Peaks of Shirouma (one of Japan's three great snow gullies)
8. Ushiro-Tateyama traverse (Shirouma - Karamatsu - Goryu - Kashimayari - Harinoki)
9. Kumo-no-taira and Takamagahara Onsen (Japan's most remote hot spring, at the source of the Kurobe river)
10. Tateyama Kurobe Alpine Route (Ogizawa - Kurobe Dam - Murodo, reaching 2,450m without any hiking)

### Famous Sights

* Kamikochi (Kappa Bridge, Taisho Pond, Myojin Pond - Japan's signature alpine resort valley, closed to private cars)
* Kurobe Gorge and Kurobe Dam (the Kuroyon dam, 186m, the tallest arch dam in Japan)
* Murodo and the Yuki-no-Otani snow corridor (walls of snow up to 20m at the spring opening), with Mikurigaike Pond and Jigokudani
* Shin-Hotaka Ropeway (Japan's only double-decker cable car, rising to Nishi-Hotaka-guchi at 2,156m)
* Happo-one and Happo Pond, which mirrors the Three Peaks of Shirouma
* Matsumoto Castle (an original keep and one of Japan's five National Treasure castles)
* The Sanmachi old town of Hida-Takayama and the Oku-Hida hot spring villages (Hirayu, Shin-Hotaka)
* Omachi Onsen village and the three Nishina lakes (Aoki, Nakatsuna, Kizaki)
* The Kurobe Gorge Railway (Unazuki to Keyakidaira) and the sea cliffs of Oyashirazu on the Sea of Japan

### Historical Events

* Tateyama worship: since the Nara and Heian periods Tateyama has been counted among Japan's Three Holy Mountains. The temples of Ashikuraji and Iwakuraji at its foot were the centre of the cult, which developed the Nunobashi Kanjo-e rite for women, and Edo-period Tateyama preachers spread the Tateyama Mandala across the country.
* In 1881 the British mining engineer William Gowland coined the name "Japanese Alps" for the Hida Mountains, and in 1896 the British missionary Walter Weston published *Mountaineering and Exploring in the Japanese Alps*, introducing modern mountaineering to Japan. A memorial to Weston stands in Kamikochi, where the Weston Festival is held every June.
* The 1915 eruption of Mount Yake dammed the Azusa river with mudflows and created Taisho Pond, now one of Kamikochi's best-known sights.
* 1956-1963: construction of the Kurobe No. 4 hydroelectric dam deep in the roadless Kurobe Gorge required some ten million worker-days and cost 171 lives; it was later dramatised in the film *Kurobe no Taiyo*, and its construction road forms part of today's Tateyama Kurobe Alpine Route.
* 1963: the Mount Yakushi disaster, in which all 13 students of the Aichi University alpine club died in a winter storm - one of Japan's worst post-war mountaineering accidents, after which university club winter climbing was thoroughly reformed.
* 1934: designation of Chubu-Sangaku National Park, one of Japan's earliest national parks. In 2016 Japan made 11 August a national holiday, "Mountain Day", and held the first national ceremony in Kamikochi.
