### Historical Events

* 1543 - The arrival of firearms: a Chinese ship carrying Portuguese merchants drifted ashore at Tanegashima; the island's lord, Tanegashima Tokitaka, purchased matchlock guns and had craftsmen reproduce them, marking the introduction of Western firearms to Japan and profoundly reshaping Sengoku-era warfare - Japanese matchlocks are still called "tanegashima" today.
* Edo-period Yakusugi tribute system: since rice cultivation was difficult on Yakushima, the Satsuma Domain from the 17th century onward allowed islanders to pay their annual tax in "hiraki" cedar shingles split from Yakusugi wood instead of rice; the resulting large-scale logging inadvertently left behind many giant stumps later rediscovered by generations to come.
* 1966 - Discovery of Jomon Sugi: forestry workers discovered this giant Yakusugi cedar, estimated at 2,000 to 7,200 years old, deep in the mountains; the news sparked a wave of conservation interest and tourism, becoming a key catalyst for Yakushima's later World Heritage bid.
* 1993 - Yakushima inscribed as a UNESCO World Natural Heritage Site: alongside Shirakami-Sanchi, one of Japan's first World Natural Heritage sites, recognizing the ecological value of its old-growth Yakusugi forest and unique vertical vegetation zones.
* 1969 - Tanegashima Space Center opens: the starting point of Japan's space program, and still JAXA's principal rocket launch site today.

### Princess Mononoke

* In Hayao Miyazaki's 1997 Studio Ghibli film "Princess Mononoke" (Mononoke Hime), the primeval forest inhabited by the Deer God (Shishigami) was modeled on Yakushima. During production, Miyazaki visited the island repeatedly, staying in Anbo, and only began storyboarding after he and the crew had hiked extensively through the forest; art director Kazuo Oga also led a location-sketching trip to the island.
* Deep along the Kusugawa Trail in Shiratani Unsuikyo, the "Moss Forest" (once nicknamed the "Princess Mononoke Forest") is considered the setting closest to the film: moss-covered granite boulders amid old-growth Yakusugi forest, wreathed in mist year-round, and designated one of Japan's "Precious Moss Forests" by the Bryological Society of Japan.
* Shiratani Unsuikyo has three main routes: the Yayoi Sugi route (about 1 hour), the Bugyo Sugi route (about 3 hours), and the Taikoiwa round trip (about 6 hours); highlights along the way include the Satsuki Suspension Bridge, Kuguri Sugi, Shiratani Hut, and Tsuji Pass.
* From the Moss Forest, continuing about 30 minutes past Tsuji Pass leads up to Taikoiwa, with views over the Oku-dake peaks and endless primeval forest - spring cherry blossoms and autumn foliage each lending their own seasonal character.
