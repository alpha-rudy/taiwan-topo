### Famous Peaks

This area covers the central-western section of the Greater Caucasus, including Europe's highest peak Mount Elbrus and its surrounding valleys, extending east to Nalchik, the capital of the Kabardino-Balkar Republic:
* Mount Elbrus (5,642m): Europe's highest peak, a dormant volcano with twin summits — the west summit (5,642m) is slightly higher than the east summit (5,621m). Permanently snow-capped, it is one of the Seven Summits.
* Ushba (4,710m): Its steep, twin-fanged silhouette has earned it the nickname "Matterhorn of the Caucasus"; a highly technical climbing objective.
* Shkhelda (4,320m): Located in the Prielbrusye area next to Elbrus, with several glacier routes.

### Famous Trekking Routes

* Elbrus Standard Route:
  * Starts from the Gara-Bashi cable car station (3,847m) on the south side, passes Lenz Rocks, and continues to the summit; a non-technical high-altitude climb.
* Prielbrusye Trek:
  * Follows the Baksan Valley through several side valleys, offering spectacular panoramic views of Elbrus.

### Famous Sights

* Cheget Ski Resort: Located upstream in the Baksan Valley with a face-on view of Elbrus; a famous Russian alpine ski resort.
* Lake Terskol: A clear alpine lake near Terskol village, reflecting Elbrus's twin summits.
* Dzily-Su Hot Springs: Natural sulphur springs on Elbrus's north side, with distant views of the north-side glaciers.
* Chegem Gorge & Waterfalls: On the eastern side of the map, a deep gorge with sheer cliffs; the falls freeze into ice cascades in winter, one of Kabardino-Balkaria's best-known natural sights.
* Gizhgit / Tyrnyauz: Tyrnyauz, the main town in the eastern part of the map, is the gateway from Nalchik to Elbrus; the Baksan Valley highway leads directly to Terskol.
* Nalchik: Capital of the Kabardino-Balkar Republic, population roughly 250,000; the main transport hub for reaching the Elbrus region, with an airport and train station.

### Historical Events

* 1829 - First ascent of Elbrus's east summit: Khillar Khachirov, a guide for a Russian scientific expedition, made the first ascent, marking the start of modern mountaineering in the Caucasus.
* 1874 - First ascent of the west (main) summit: a team led by British mountaineer F. Crauford Grove reached the top, confirming the west summit as the mountain's true high point.
* 1942 - Battle of the Caucasus: during WWII, Germany's "Edelweiss" mountain division captured Elbrus and planted Nazi flags on both summits in a highly symbolic act; in 1943 Soviet Red Army mountain troops retook the peak, removed the Nazi flags, and raised the Soviet flag.
* As Europe's highest peak, Elbrus has long been included on the Seven Summits challenge list, drawing mountaineers from around the world.
