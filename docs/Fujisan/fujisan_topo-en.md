Sacred Summit - Fujisan

## Map Coverage

* Latitude range: N34.30 ~ N35.95
* Longitude range: E137.69 ~ E139.55

### Famous Peaks

* Mount Fuji
  * Mount Fuji (3,776m) - Japan's highest peak; inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2013

* Hyakumeizan (100 Famous Mountains of Japan) near Mount Fuji
  * Mount Kumotori (2,017m)
  * Mount Tanzawa (1,567m)
  * Kobushi-ga-dake (2,475m)
  * Mount Daibosatsu (2,057m)
  * Mount Kinpu (2,599m)
  * Mount Mizugaki (2,230m)
  * Mount Amagi (1,406m)
  * Kiso-Koma-ga-take (2,956m)

* The following Hyakumeizan lie in the Southern Alps (Akaishi Mountains)
  * Kita-dake (3,193m) - Japan's second-highest peak
  * Kaikoma-ga-take (2,967m)
  * Senjo-ga-take (3,033m)
  * Mount Houou, Jizo-dake (2,764m)
  * Ai-no-dake (3,190m) - Japan's third-highest peak
  * Shiomi-dake (3,047m)
  * Warusawa-dake (3,141m)
  * Akaishi-dake (3,121m)
  * Hijiri-dake (3,013m)
  * Kō-dake (2,592m)

* Other well-known sightseeing spots and peaks
  * Tokyo: Takao-yama, Mitake-san/Ōtake-san, Mitsutoge-yama
  * Yamanashi: Shōsen-kyō/Yajiro-dake, Irikasa-yama, Minobu-yama, Fuji Five Lakes/Mitsu-toge-yama
  * Kanagawa: Hakone/Hakone-Komagatake
  * Shizuoka: Izu Peninsula, Numazu Trail

### Famous Trekking Routes

1. Mount Fuji climbing routes
    * Yoshida Trail: the most heavily used route, with the best access to the 5th Station trailhead and the densest cluster of mountain huts
    * Fujinomiya Trail: the shortest route to the summit, starting from the highest trailhead
    * Subashiri Trail: on the descent, hikers can take the "Osunabashiri" scree run for a fast, scenic way down
    * Gotemba Trail: the longest route with the gentlest gradient, and the least crowded
    * Ohachi-meguri: a circuit around the crater rim after reaching the top, leading to the true summit at Kengamine

2. Southern Alps (Akaishi Mountains) traverses
    * Shirane Sanzan Traverse (Kita-dake - Ai-no-dake - Nōtori-dake, via Hirogawara and Kita-dake Hut)
    * Kaikoma - Senjo Traverse (starting from Kitazawa Pass, bagging two Hyakumeizan in one trip)
    * Hōō Sanzan Traverse (Jizo-dake - Kannon-dake - Yakushi-dake, starting from Yashajin Pass)
    * Southern Alps Grand Traverse (Shiomi-dake - Warusawa-dake - Akaishi-dake - Hijiri-dake, a multi-day classic of Japanese high-mountain traversing)

3. Trails near the Greater Tokyo area
    * Tanzawa Main Ridge Traverse (Ōkura Ridge - Tō-no-dake - Tanzawa-san - Hiru-ga-dake)
    * Takao-san - Jinba-san Traverse (a beginner-friendly Hyakumeizan-adjacent route near Tokyo)
    * Mount Daibosatsu - Daibosatsu Pass loop (a famous viewpoint for Mount Fuji)
    * Mitake-san - Ōtake-san Traverse (a signature route of the Okutama area)
    * Amagi-san Traverse (the spine of the Izu Peninsula, a Hyakumeizan)
    * Numazu Alps Traverse (a low-elevation Shizuoka route with views over Suruga Bay and Mount Fuji)
    * Hakone Outer Rim Traverse (Kintoki-san - Myōjin-ga-take, circling Lake Ashi)

### Famous Sights

* Fuji Five Lakes: Kawaguchiko, Yamanakako, Saiko, Shōjiko, and Motosuko - the view of Mount Fuji reflected in Lake Motosu is the scene printed on the back of the 1,000-yen banknote
* Component sites of the Mount Fuji World Heritage listing: Fujisan Hongū Sengen Taisha, Oshino Hakkai, Shiraito Falls, and 25 designated sites in total (inscribed by UNESCO in 2013)
* Hakone: Lake Ashi, Owakudani, Hakone Shrine, and its hot-spring towns; sightseeing boats on Lake Ashi offer views of Mount Fuji's reflection
* Shōsen-kyō: Yamanashi's signature gorge scenery, one of Japan's three great Special Places of Scenic Beauty
* Izu Peninsula: Jōgasaki Coast, Shuzenji Onsen; designated a UNESCO Global Geopark in 2018
* Gotemba: Tokinosumika and Gotemba Premium Outlets, a popular spot for views of Mount Fuji's eastern flank

### Historical Events

* Fuji faith and Shugendō: since the Heian period, Mount Fuji has been revered as a sacred Shugendō mountain; during the Edo period the "Fuji-kō" devotional societies spread nationwide, and organized pilgrimage climbs became a popular custom among commoners.
* 1707 - The Hōei eruption: Mount Fuji's most recent recorded eruption, occurring 49 days after the great Hōei earthquake; ash fell as far as Edo (present-day Tokyo), and the Hōei crater is still visible on the mountain's flank today.
* 1860 - Ascent by Sir Rutherford Alcock: the British envoy's climb is the first recorded ascent of Mount Fuji by a Westerner.
* 1872 - The Meiji government lifts the ban on women climbing: Mount Fuji had long been considered off-limits to women; the Meiji Restoration formally opened the mountain to female climbers, symbolizing the loosening of a traditional religious taboo amid Japan's modernization.
* 1905 - Founding of the Japanese Alpine Club: established by British missionary Walter Weston together with Kojima Usui and others, ushering in Japan's modern era of high-mountain traversing; exploration and development of the Southern Alps peaks also began around this time.
* 2013 - Mount Fuji inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site: listed under the title "Fujisan, sacred place and source of artistic inspiration," cementing its status as one of Japan's most important cultural landmarks.
